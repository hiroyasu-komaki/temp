PART VII　TOOLING

Chosen last — the rule for selecting a tool

Tool selection comes after the measurement design is settled.
Reverse the order and you will measure only what the tool can measure.

39The problems no tool solves form a distinct cluster

Classify the general problems of skill management by whether a tool solves them, and the ones no tool solves form a cluster.

Problem no tool solvesDomain it belongs to
No incentive to keep data currentOperating design
Absence of ownershipOrganisational design
Resistance rooted in feeling surveilledCulture and accountability
These are problems of implementation design, not of tool selection. Why we collect the data, what it will and will not be used for, who holds definitional authority — settle none of these and either product produces the same outcome.

Selecting a product first therefore will not move our problem at all.
40The single selection rule

Because the measurement design runs on two tracks, the domains that go into the product and those that do not are already separated.

The single rule
Do not put overlay A (R4) into the product's taxonomy.
There is no disqualifying criterion. Observe this rule and the rest can be decided on how well the product handles the base layer.

With that rule in place, most of what was previously listed as a product requirement disappears.

Previous requirementResolution
Can proficiency labels be replaced with three levels?Not needed. R4 is not in the product
Can company-specific categories be created?Not needed. Same reason
Can "verified / unverified" be shown alongside?Not needed. Unverified is expressed as a blank cell
All three arose from trying to force R4 into the product. Stop forcing it and they disappear.
