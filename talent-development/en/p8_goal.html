PART VIII　END STATE

What "solved" looks like, and what must be decided

What state we are aiming for, what artefacts should exist in it, and who has to decide what.

42What "solved" looks like
① Failure of perception, solved
any function × site can be answered from the record

A state in which "can this domain be executed in-house, or does it stop without the vendor?" can be answered from the record.

  • Every item in scope has been determined as "can execute / can evaluate / custodial only"
  • That determination rests on traces — in-house estimates, rejection records, alternatives proposed, triage hypotheses — rather than on self-report, such that a third party following the records arrives at the same determination
  • Domains with no traces and no possible determination are output as "unverified", distinctly from "low level"
  • The disconnection between measurement results and compensation is in writing, so that reporting the situation accurately carries no penalty
  • The output is presented in a form that does not invite reading a stage difference as a capability difference, a poor rating of veterans, or a cost-cutting programme
What does not count as solved: better values on the inventory. In the first round, large numbers of "custodial only" and "unverified" entries are normal, and their appearance is the evidence that it worked. The criterion is not that values are good but that they are correct.
② Absence of incentive to align, solved
a role exists that cannot be discharged unless the scales align

A state in which a role exists that cannot be discharged unless the scales align, and that role is actually making the determinations.

  • A single body holds authority over the vocabulary, granularity and criteria of skill definitions
  • That body's remit is not "aligning" as such, but a separate accountability that cannot be met unless things are aligned
  • Determination of level is performed by that body rather than by the sites
  • A revision history exists for the anchor cases, so that sites can verify the criteria have not been moved arbitrarily
  • All three sites record in the same form and vocabulary, and divergence between sites has been detected and there is a record of it being resolved
  • "Can we deploy someone from Japan in North America?" has become a decidable question
What does not count as solved: people actually moving between sites. What is solved is the ability to make the determination; execution still faces visas, language, compensation structures and individual intent. Nor does appointing a coordinator count — if that person is not inconvenienced, only a title has been created.
③ Loss of design-rationale knowledge, solved
losing it unknowingly no longer happens
  • A register exists, and is kept current, of who holds the design rationale for which system
  • Single-holder cases have been identified and counted
  • Among single-holder cases, those where the holder's expected remaining tenure makes the deadline pressing have been identified and prioritised
  • For high-priority cases, transfer has been assigned as work and time has been allocated
  • For the person transferring, it is assured that handing over the rationale does not disadvantage them
  • Where transfer is complete, there are two or more holders
What does not count as solved: every system having multiple holders. That is not realistic in cost or headcount terms, and as a target it would never be met. Solved is not zero single-holder cases but single-holder cases identified and the time-critical ones addressed.
