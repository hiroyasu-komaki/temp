PART II　POSITION

The starting point for accumulation — three sites, development stages, three root problems

Strategy B is a strategy of accumulation. You cannot begin without knowing where you are starting from.
This part fixes that starting position.

07The three sites

The scope is the IT organisation across three sites, roughly 200 people in total. The large majority are located in Japan.

Japan — headquarters function
A headquarters function that has supported the business for decades, with people deployed across the entire value chain. At the same time it has never secured enough headcount to cover every domain, and has been dependent on vendors over the long term.
North America — from sales to manufacturing
Originally a sales site; IT staffing is being built up for the shift to manufacturing. Conditions typical of a start-up phase have produced short-cycle turnover of personnel.
Europe — supporting sales
The function is support for a sales site, and the staff are closer to sales support than to IT specialists.
Common structure across the three sites — each site has its own head of IT, and each of them sets that site's IT strategy, hiring, and capability strategy.
08Development stages, and the three principles that follow

Rather than treating the differences between the three sites as local circumstances, we place them within a general framework.

The general model

Overseas sites of multinational companies generally expand their role through the following stages. As the stage rises, more functions are placed locally, and with them the very kind of IT capability required changes.

StageRole of the siteNature of local IT staff
Stage 1 Export / agencyNo own site in the marketNone needed. HQ handles everything
Stage 2 Sales siteSelling into the local marketSales-centred. IT are users of HQ systems
Stage 3 Functional expansionAdding functions adjacent to salesNot IT specialists but general business-support staff
Stage 4 Production / operationsLocal manufacturing and operations beginSpecialist IT staff urgently needed, including for regulatory work. Organisational accumulation is still thin
Stage 5 Full value chainAn autonomous site holding all functions including R&D and planningBroad specialist staff in long tenure, and knowledge accumulates
What the model shows is a difference of stage, not of merit. A Stage 2 site having no specialist IT staff is not a shortfall — it is that the stage does not require them. Stage transitions are not irreversible, but they cannot be skipped. The move to Stage 4 always produces an unstable period in which "specialists are needed but the organisational pattern does not yet exist".

The three principles that follow

Principle 1 — do not apply one standard uniformly across the three sites
so that a difference of stage is not misread as a difference of capability

Apply the same yardstick to organisations at different stages and the younger site is uniformly reported as "immature", while the mature site is reported as "over-broad and inefficient". Neither is a comparison of reality.

The constraint running through the whole framework: what is shared is the definition of the scale; what is not shared is the target value and the scope of application.
Principle 2 — "development stage" and "capability level in a given domain" are different things
the most easily overlooked point

The stage model explains the range of functions a site holds, not the level of capability within any individual domain.

Manufacturing IT in North America is the concrete example. The site as a whole is in transition, but within manufacturing IT it has completed construction against a different standard from Japan's and has authored its own SOPs and technical standards. An organisation that can author the standard can execute in that domain — objective evidence of level that does not rely on self-assessment.

Japan, meanwhile, has supported existing plants for many years, but that is capability against a different standard. Not inferior in absolute terms — a different reference standard.

The assumption that "Japan is advanced and the overseas sites are behind" does not hold. Supplier and recipient can swap roles domain by domain.
Principle 3 — different problems require different interventions
a company-wide numerical target fits none of the three sites
SiteCentre of gravity for intervention
JapanDevelopment (hiring does not fill design-rationale knowledge)
North AmericaDesigning the capability to be placed (whether it enters the same structure as Japan is still open)
EuropeChanging what is measured (do not apply the same yardstick to a dissimilar object)
Where not to use this model — individual assessment (stage is a property of the organisation); guaranteeing a future destination (being in transition does not imply arrival); domain-level decisions (site level is too coarse). Capability level within a domain has to be measured separately.
09Three root problems

Our problems resolve into three root problems. Who solves them is left open; the timing and method belong to whoever owns each problem.

① The organisation cannot perceive its own state
recoverable

What is measured and what is real are misaligned in two directions at once.

  • What staff have actually been doing — translating requirements, acceptance judgement, verifying the reasonableness of effort estimates, design-rationale knowledge, internal coordination — does not exist in the standard taxonomy
  • Conversely, the implementation, design and build capabilities that are in the standard taxonomy are quite likely not held in-house, after long-term vendor dependence

More serious is the nature of the error. From the fact "I have owned this domain for fifteen years", the conclusion "I can execute in this domain" follows automatically. There is no bad faith and no falsehood; ownership and execution are simply conflated. And because everyone in the organisation shares the same frame of reference, peer review does not correct it either.

The skill inventory therefore conceals exactly what we most need to know — can this domain be executed in-house, or does it stop without the vendor? This sits upstream of "visualisation that goes nowhere": the object of visualisation is itself wrong.
② No role has any incentive to align
recoverable

Each of the three sites has its own head of IT, and each sets that site's capability strategy. Every one of them is discharging their role correctly. And still the scales do not align.

This differs structurally from the usual "absence of ownership".

That case is "nobody is doing it"; this case is "everyone is doing it, separately." Appointing a coordinator therefore does not close it — for that person, nothing stops when the scales fail to align. The organisation is inconvenienced; the individual is not.
  • Comparing and deploying people across sites has become undeterminable as a matter of principle, not of will
  • Five years of talent development has not been one activity but three parallel, separate activities
③ What is being lost has a deadline
irreversible — the only item with a real deadline

Single-person holding of design-rationale knowledge — why a given system was built the way it was.

  • Where ① and ② are recoverable, this alone is irreversible
  • The moment the holder leaves, it cannot be restored by hiring, redeployment or external procurement
  • And at present, no list exists of who holds what alone. Producing one is estimated at about half a day
This is the only item with a deadline that actually exists.

Sequence is set by the presence of a deadline, not by depth of dependency.
C6 — producing the register of design-rationale holders, half a day — comes first. Everything else is recoverable; this alone cannot be undone.
