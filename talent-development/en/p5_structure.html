PART V　STRUCTURE

The vessel that spreads accumulation — definitional authority, function lines, mutual cover

The acceleration in Strategy B depends on network effects. Unless knowledge circulates across sites, the later acceleration never arrives.
This part designs the vessel that lets it circulate.

25Why move the subject

For each site's head of IT, a definition that works within their own site is enough to discharge the role. No role generates any incentive to align with the others.

This is an absence of incentive, not an absence of an owner. All three heads are discharging their roles correctly. And still the scales do not align.

MeasureWho is inconvenienced when the scales do not alignResult
Set up a cross-cutting committee or CoENobody. The committee's agenda proceeds either wayNo alignment

Create someone whose work does not run unless the scales alignThat person is. Every dayAlignment
The substance of the function-line idea is not a tidier org chart. It is bringing into existence a role that cannot be discharged unless the scales align.

The line head holds definitional authority, anchor selection and determination simultaneously. An unmeasured judge leaves a self-scoring structure in place.

A line head at "custodial only" is exactly the state of a dotted line with no authority behind it — that is, function lines have been established and the organisation has reverted to the unaligned state (C4). Being able to detect that is the reason for measuring line heads.

Who determines it — the line heads assess each other

At first sight this contradicts the earlier statement that peer visibility does not work against type ③ error, since colleagues share the same frame. Here it does not contradict.

Why peer assessment works in this case
Line heads hold different functions. It is precisely because they do not share a frame that peer assessment works.
Type ③ requires that assessor and assessed share the same frame of reference. The infrastructure line head and the data line head do not hold the "fifteen years means expertise" misconception about each other's domains.
It also helps that the object of assessment is calibration, a shared activity. Whether anchor revision history exists can be checked without expertise in the other function.

30What mutual cover is actually cleaning up after

Mutual cover is not a solution to an existing problem. It is the clean-up for a vulnerability created by choosing a functional organisation.

A functional organisation divides a site by the number of functions. With five lines, North America and Europe hold one or two people per function. If that one person leaves, that function stops at that site.

This vulnerability does not exist today. North American IT staff are not divided by function; a small number cover a wide area thinly. The single-point dependency is created structurally the moment function lines are established.

Chapters 25–29 and 30–32 are not independent proposals. The latter is the carrying cost of the former, and becomes unnecessary if function lines are not adopted. Presenting the headcount increase as "new investment" makes approval hard; presenting it as "the cost of a functional organisation" allows it to be judged together with the function-line decision.

C does not work at small sites. For one person in North America or Europe to hold five functions is identical to being "custodial only" in all five. Redundancy appears to increase, while what actually increases is the number of states that must be detected.

B is the strongest counter-argument to D. External redundancy is the cheapest. If the vendor has two people, losing one does not stop the work. And it can be bought under contract.

B has one condition, however — without someone inside who can evaluate, external redundancy cannot be verified. If you cannot judge whether "we have two people on it" is true, that is not redundancy but a claim of redundancy.

The question this raises
Is securing the evaluation level plus external redundancy (B) not cheaper than mutual cover (D)?
B presupposes that the evaluation level exists. Put the other way round, secure the evaluation level and redundancy of execution can be bought externally. The next chapter answers the question directly.

Being classified as ② means being standardised and documented. And work that is standardised and documented is, at the same time, work that can be placed outside. The moment the ③→② conversion succeeds, the domain also becomes a candidate for B.

The justification for D therefore cannot be completed within an argument about capability. D beats B only where one of the following can be asserted.

#Reason to choose DNature
1Regulation or audit forbids delegating the judgement outsideExternal condition. Limited in scope, but real in a regulated industry
2Use it as the route for moving design-rationale knowledge to North AmericaStrategic judgement. See below
3We do not want to place it outside (insourcing policy)Strategic judgement. Not an argument about capability

Reason 2 is in fact the strongest — and this is where Strategy B connects

Japan has decades of accumulated design-rationale and domain knowledge, and that has partially compensated for external dependence. North America has no such compensating stock. Placed in the same structure, it would be more fragile than Japan.

Mutual cover is the institutional route for moving that compensating stock. Rather than simply buying redundancy, the requirement that "the other site must be able to take this over" forces documentation and explanation on the Japan side.

Outsourcing does not produce this — the vendor takes over the work, but the design-rationale knowledge leaves the company and nothing remains in North America.

Where mutual cover stands
D's real benefit is not redundancy but being a forcing device for knowledge transfer. If redundancy is the only aim, B is cheaper.
This is our implementation of what the second figure calls "cross-border collaboration for Strategy B" — the cross-border SECI model and communities of practice (CoP).
Turning the knowledge spiral requires shared practice across sites. Mutual cover is the mechanism that forces it, through organisational structure.

How to determine that it works

"They are at the same level" cannot be determined on paper. What is required is equivalence, and equivalence cannot be confirmed through vocabulary.

Once a year, actually exchange part of the ② domain. North America takes over work Japan has been doing, and Japan takes over work North America has been doing. Whether the handover succeeded is itself the determination. The exercise leaves records — change records, artefacts, review records — which are primary and secondary evidence in their own right.
Confirming that it works generates the evidence for next year's measurement. It is not extra work for calibration; it is part of running the measurement.
32Insourcing policy, and calculating α

② is insourced by default

The previous chapter showed that D's justification depends on a separate judgement: insourcing policy. That judgement is stated here.

Position adopted
Work classified as ② location-free and transferable is, as a rule, held at two sites in-house. Where external redundancy is chosen instead, a reason must be given for that domain.
That is, insourcing is the default and externalisation the exception. The claim that "B is cheaper" is not withdrawn. This is a declaration that we choose to insource knowing it is more expensive.

Relationship with "we set no numerical insourcing target"

That position stands. There is no contradiction.

Set the rule and let the ratio emerge as a result. What is not set is the number, not the policy. Hold all ② at two sites without stating the rule and you are executing an insourcing policy silently.

What the decision simplifies

Definition of α
Before(share classified as ②) × (share of ② judged to be insourced)
AfterShare classified as ②

The second term becomes 1. α is therefore fixed by the classification work alone and waits on no further management decision.

Identifying ② = fixing α = fixing the North American demand figure. The ①②③ classification is an inventory of Japan's work (mutual cover has Japan as principal and North America as secondary), so Japan can draft it and the other two sites review — work can start without waiting for line heads to be appointed.

The main cost variables — two paradoxes

  • The share of ③ is itself what suppresses cost. A substantial part of Japan's existing system estate is likely to be ③. ③ is outside mutual cover, so headcount rises only to the extent that ③ is standardised and documented into ②. In other words, the more you standardise, the more it costs — a paradox that must be built into the plan
  • The internal split of applications (global-common vs site-specific) drives cost directly. Site-specific applications lean towards ① and fall outside scope. That boundary is the largest single variable in Δ
Precondition — retention in North America comes first. Execution level does not accumulate in an organisation where people do not stay; if the people developed are replaced, the development is reset each time. A plan that skips this precondition leaks investment continuously.

What mutual cover does not solve

  • It is not a cost reduction. It secures redundancy and increases total headcount. Declare cost reduction as the aim and it will inevitably become another name for headcount reduction
  • It does not substitute for standardisation and documentation. Without converting ③ into ②, aligning capability will not produce a handover. That is a separate exercise carrying substantial effort of its own
  • It does not guarantee that placement can be executed. Regulation, visas, language, compensation structures and individual intent remain outside skills
