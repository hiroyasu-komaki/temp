Drawing the target line — in what unit is demand written?

The procedure for drawing the required-capability line (the orange line).
That line is not one line but three. A form that says "record required headcount by function × site × role" applies only to the base layer.

Why overlay A cannot be written as headcount

R4 asks whether someone can judge, with reasons, that an external estimate or deliverable is sound. The meaning of this capability changes between zero and one person in a domain, and barely changes between one and three.

Secure the evaluation level and redundancy of execution can be bought externally. R4 therefore does not scale with volume of execution. Whether there are ten projects or a hundred, what matters is whether someone can judge. Ask for "required headcount: 3" and you get a meaningless number, mechanically apportioned from execution volume.

Why overlay B is not even demand

Design-rationale knowledge cannot be created by development, hiring or external procurement, because it exists only in the past.

Create a row for overlay B in the demand table and you produce, every year, a demand that cannot be filled. What can be written for overlay B is not "required number of holders" but "current number of holders" — an observation on the supply side, not a demand.

22The basis for deriving demand differs by site

Apply Principle 1 from Part II to the demand side — what is shared is the definition of the scale; what is not shared is the target value and the scope of application.

Demand is a target value. It is therefore not aligned across sites. What is aligned is only the form and the unit.
Japan — do not build demand up from workload
Stage 5, with people deployed across the whole value chain

Build required headcount up naively from workload and the number will greatly exceed the current establishment. That cannot be executed: the state of "not enough people to cover every domain" has no realistic prospect of being resolved by headcount increases.

It is therefore treated not as a problem to be solved but as a constraint every measure must obey. Demand that ignores the constraint becomes a machine that emits "we need more people" — an unexecutable gap — every year. Once that happens, the supply–demand gap itself stops being read.

Japan's demand is therefore written not from workload but from "the range that cannot be placed outside".

Written this way, demand does not become a request for headcount. What it produces is a decidable gap: "against the range we chose to hold in-house, do we have enough people who can execute?"

And for Japan, the most important output is not the base layer but the coverage status of overlay A. If nobody can evaluate the domains that were placed outside, then external dependence is not under control.

North America — the only site whose demand can be back-calculated from a date
Stage 3→4; new manufacturing site targeted to be operational in 2027

Of the three sites, only North America has demand with a date attached.

North American demand ＝ steady-state headcount required at go-live nN′ ＋ mutual-cover increment Σ (nN′ − nN) × α

α is the share of work classified as ② location-free and transferable. Because ② is insourced by default, α is fixed by the classification work alone — no further management decision is required.

Identifying ② = fixing α = fixing the North American demand figure (→ Part V, Ch. 32)
One condition applies: execution level does not accumulate in an organisation where people do not stay. North America has short-cycle turnover, so fixing demand will not close the gap unless retention is resolved. Numbers will appear in the demand table, but they will not be an achievable target.
Europe — do not ask for headcount
Stage 2–3; the question is whether IT should be a separate organisation at all

Create a Europe column in a function × site × role table and whoever fills it must choose between writing zero and inventing a number. Neither reflects reality. For Europe, therefore, insert a prior step before headcount.

"Do not hold it" is a design decision, not an omission. Record it as demand of zero and it will be emitted as "unmet" year after year. A field is needed in which not holding the function is recorded explicitly.
23Demand determines the scope of supply-side measurement

Easily overlooked: in sequence, demand comes first.

Of Europe we said that measuring on the same framework leaves everyone pinned at "custodial only" with no information. That was written as a measurement-design problem, but the cause is on the demand side. Measure a function for which no demand has been defined and of course supply looks like zero. Because the absence of demand is not recorded, it is emitted as a deficiency.

Operating consequence
Function × site combinations with no demand are not measured.
The scope of supply-side measurement is determined only once the demand table is fixed. This also matters for effort — 3 sites × 5 functions × 5 roles is 75 cells if measured exhaustively, but only some of them carry demand.

For overlay B in particular, by the time a gap appears the options are already limited. The only variable is whether the holder is still with the company.

Who writes, and who calibrates

The demand figures are written by the site head of IT, not the function line head (personnel authority and budget stay with the site — → Part V, Ch. 27).

But if the sites also own the vocabulary, unit and granularity, the three sites will write demand at three different granularities. "Applications: 5" and "core-system maintenance 2 / Web 1 / data integration 1" cannot be summed or compared. This is structurally the same problem as C4 on the supply side.
