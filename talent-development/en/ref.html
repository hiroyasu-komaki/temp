Solid and dotted lines — what "reporting line" actually refers to

"Solid line / dotted line", used in Part V, Ch. 26, is not an academic term. It is shorthand that arose spontaneously in practice.

To argue precisely, the phrase has to be translated into "which parts of the bundle of authority are held by whom". Used untranslated, two people can agree while talking about different things.
Practitioner's termCorresponding academic conceptSource
A single solid line onlyUnity of commandFayol (1916)
Solid plus dottedDual reporting / integrating roleGalbraith (1973)
Two solid linesMatrix organisationGalbraith (1971); Davis & Lawrence (1977)
Where this framework sits
Option (c) corresponds to an integrating role. It is not a matrix organisation.
Only definitional authority moves; personnel authority, budget and day-to-day direction stay with the site. No second boss is created.

Why a dotted line feels like an exception

Fayol listed fourteen principles of management, one of them unity of command.

A subordinate should receive orders from one superior only.
The reason is simple — when two people give contradictory instructions, the subordinate has no means of resolving them. The responsibility for resolving the contradiction is pushed onto the party without the authority.

The dotted line is a deliberate exception to that principle. The intuition that it is exceptional is correct — it has been positioned that way for a hundred years.

Three characteristic failure modes of the dotted line

ModeContent
① A dotted line with no authority
"advisory only"		Nothing actually moves. A line head at "custodial only" is exactly this state (→ Part V, Ch. 28)
② Responsibility for contradiction falls to the subordinateWhen two people give contradictory instructions, resolution is pushed onto the party without authority
③ The dotted line becomes a solid one in practiceWhat was moved as definitional authority turns into de facto command
The four conditions for transfer (→ Part V, Ch. 29) exist to avoid these three. Condition 1 blocks mode ③, condition 2 blocks mode ①, and the split of authority in option (c) blocks mode ② by construction.
